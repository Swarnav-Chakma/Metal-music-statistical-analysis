# Data audit 01: feature matrix quality and confounders

Generated by `analysis/01_data_audit.py` from `/Users/swarnavchakma/metal_data` (X.npy SHA-256 `79a6605a488feee0…`). Rerun the script to regenerate this file; do not edit it by hand.

Nothing in the dataset was modified. Z-scores, correlations and group means below are computed in memory as diagnostics only; no scaling, model or metric has been chosen.

## Headline findings

- **Integrity passes.** 5,874 rows × 2,675 columns, all finite; metadata, feature names and matrix align; 5,874 rows re-derived from raw JSON match exactly; no duplicate IDs or repeated rows; train and validation share no album (3,633 albums).
- **Same-artist candidates are the largest threat to valid scores.** A validation query and a training candidate by the same artist share a subgenre 95% of the time, versus 8.5% for a random pair. 1,411 of 2,766 validation queries have at least one same-artist training candidate, and 248 have ten or more same-artist, same-subgenre candidates, enough to fill a top-10 list on artist identity alone.
- **Unscaled distances would be meaningless.** Column standard deviations span 10^-15.85 to 10^17.16; `lowlevel.melbands_kurtosis.*` alone carries essentially all raw variance. 1,719 columns have |skew| > 2.
- **364 columns are constant or exact duplicates** (34 constant; 330 further exact duplicates, mostly the mirrored halves of the symmetric MFCC/GFCC covariance matrices). After also removing near-constant columns and the track-length proxy, 2,262 columns would remain.
- **Extractor version is a measurable confounder.** Within subgenres, version explains > 10% of variance for 70 columns (max 0.30), and the share of 2.1-beta1 recordings varies by subgenre from 6% to 35%.
- **No feature encodes IDs or split membership.** Max |Spearman ρ| with recording ID 0.050, album ID 0.053, row order 0.050; max |AUC − 0.5| for train vs validation 0.031. `rhythm.beats_count` is a track-length proxy (ρ = 0.90).
- **The archive subset is representative.** Subgenre shares match the full 30,388-recording cohort (χ² p = 0.89 train, 1.00 validation), but classes are imbalanced (largest/smallest = 6.7 train, 8.41 validation).

### 2.2 Constant, near-constant and duplicate columns

- **Constant (34)**: identical value in every row, matching the 34 listed in the extraction report. Mostly derivative statistics (dmean, dvar…) of descriptors that have one value per track, e.g. `rhythm.bpm_histogram_first_peak_bpm.dmean`, plus silence-rate maxima.
- **Exact duplicates (362, of which 312 in covariance matrices)**: each 13 × 13 MFCC/GFCC `cov`/`icov` matrix is symmetric, so 78 off-diagonal entries per matrix repeat another column. Separately, the BPM-histogram peak descriptors have mean = median = min = max per track:
  - `rhythm.bpm_histogram_first_peak_spread.max`, `rhythm.bpm_histogram_first_peak_spread.mean`, `rhythm.bpm_histogram_first_peak_spread.median`, `rhythm.bpm_histogram_first_peak_spread.min`

### 2.3 Scale, skew and extreme values

- Standard deviations range over ~33 orders of magnitude (10^-15.85 to 10^17.16). 31 columns exceed 10^6 in magnitude. Many band-energy statistics (`melbands.var`, `barkbands.dvar`…) are below 10^-6 because they are raw energies, not because they are constant.
- 1,719 non-constant columns have |skew| > 2 and 769 have |skew| > 10; 563 columns have more than 1% of values beyond 10 robust SDs from the median. Variance-type statistics (var, dvar, dvar2) and kurtosis/flatness descriptors account for most of the heavy tails.

### 3.2 Artists

- Artist IDs are present for 85.2% of training and 84.6% of validation recordings; album-artist IDs add no extra coverage (they are missing for the same files).
- Distinct artists: 1,251 in train, 858 in validation, **360 in both**. 1,411 of 2,340 validation recordings with a known artist have that artist in the training pool.
- P(same subgenre | same artist) = **0.948** vs P(same subgenre) = 0.085 for a random validation–training pair. Same-artist candidates per validation query: mean 2.8, max 33; 248 queries have ≥ 10 same-artist, same-subgenre candidates.
- Within-split same-artist pairs: 5,356 in train (495 artists with > 1 recording), 7,391 in validation (424 artists). These matter if a candidate pool ever includes the query's own split.

**Nothing blocks starting the retrieval stage**, provided the retrieval protocol includes the same-artist exclusion from the outset. The scaling/representation choice is an input to that stage, not a precondition.

## 5. Confirmed problems vs risks still to test

Confirmed (handle deterministically): constant, duplicate and near-constant columns; extreme scale differences; the track-length proxy; the strong same-artist/same-subgenre association; two duplicate-audio pairs; class imbalance.

Risks to test in the next stage: whether extractor version drives nearest-neighbour structure; how much the unfiltered artist matches inflate scores; how sensitive results are to family weighting and redundancy; residual same-artist leakage among recordings without artist IDs.
